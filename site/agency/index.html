The hardest part of selling GEO isn't the work — it's proving the work. FasterGEO turns every engagement into evidence: a diagnosis that closes the deal, tickets with acceptance criteria a machine checks, and a verification sheet that justifies the invoice.

The engagement, end to end

1 · Onboard in 5 minutes

One command derives the client's brand facts, competitors and question bank from their site — nothing invented, unknowns flagged for the kickoff call. fastergeo bootstrap --root client.com

2 · The report that closes the deal

A one-file diagnosis: what 18 Chinese and global AI engines actually say about the client — including confusions with other companies, quoted verbatim. Prospects have usually never seen their brand through AI's eyes. That moment sells.

3 · Tickets, not vague recommendations

Findings become prioritized tickets — each with a rationale and an acceptance criterion a program can check. Your team (or the client's devs) work through them like any backlog.

4 · The verification sheet that justifies the invoice

fastergeo verify re-crawls and flips tickets to done — or back to regressed. Next-period sampling shows which answers actually changed. When the client asks "did it work?", you hand them machine output, not a slide.

The 90-day delivery playbook

The industry has converged on a 90-day GEO engagement shape (baseline → entity & technical fixes → knowledge base → distribution → measurement). Every phase of it maps to a FasterGEO command with a machine-checkable exit — so each invoice line has evidence behind it.

Weeks 1–2 · Baseline & diagnosis

Fix a 30–50 question bank, sample every engine, audit the site. Purpose-aware crawler check included — 41% of sites block GPTBot without knowing it (Digital Strategy Force), and blocking a search crawler is the accident that matters.
bootstrap → sample --repeat 5 → audit → report
Exit: baseline report delivered; blocker list signed off.

Weeks 2–4 · Entity & technical fixes

Unblock search crawlers, fix shells, wire Organization JSON-LD + sameAs — entity inconsistency is the leading cause of AI misdescribing a brand, and ChatGPT Search misattributes sources in 76.5% of tested answers (Tow Center).
plan → fix → verify
Exit: P0 tickets flipped to done by re-crawl, not by claim.

Weeks 3–8 · Knowledge base & content

Answer-first pages that pass the island test, facts that trace to sources. Drafts run through the fabrication gate before the client ever sees them.
expand → outline → draft → fabcheck
Exit: issue tickets (answer-below-fold, block gaps, staleness) trending to zero.

Weeks 8–12 · Distribution

~84% of AI citations come from earned media, not brand sites (Muck Rack, 25M citations). The client's own samples name which domains AI already trusts in their category — that list is the outreach plan.
sources → publish (gate-checked) → off-site tickets
Exit: presence earned on at least one cited domain per market.

Week 12 → ongoing · Measurement

Weekly deltas, monthly share-of-voice, official-data reconciliation (GSC Gen-AI / Bing AI Performance). Single-period changes stay labeled observations — two consecutive same-direction periods make a trend. That restraint is why your numbers survive procurement review.
cycle --repeat 5 → trends → official
Exit: delta report against baseline; regressed tickets reopened automatically.

Only ~14% of marketers measure the AI channel at all (AIThinkerLab) — agencies that can prove this work are early.
